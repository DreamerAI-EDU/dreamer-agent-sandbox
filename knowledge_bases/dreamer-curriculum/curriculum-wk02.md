# 🚀 Week 2: The Problem Solver (Discover Phase)

**Focus: Critical Analysis, Prompt Iteration, and Problem-Solving**

**Primary Skills Focus: Critical Thinking**

**AI Tools: DreamerAI (for complex logic), Canva (for editing/iterating), Padlet (for organizing ideas)**


## 🕐 5-Minute Citizenship Minute: "Is AI Always Right?"

**Topic: Hallucinations and Bias**

**Context: Last week we saw AI can make cool pictures. This week, we learn that AI can lie or be wrong.**

**Discussion Script:**

Ask: "If a calculator tells you 2+2=5, is it broken? What if a teacher tells you the wrong date for a test?"

Explain: "AI is not a brain; it's a prediction machine. Sometimes it guesses wrong. This is called a 'hallucination.' Also, if the data AI learned from is unfair, AI will be unfair too."

**The Challenge: "This week, don't just trust AI. Check it. Challenge it. Fix it."**


## 📝 4-Hour Self-Learning Mission Sheet

**Mission Goal: Identify a small problem in your daily life (e.g., homework is messy, forgetting lunch money) and design a "Smart Solution" using AI.**


### Task 1: The "Bad" Prompt vs. The "Good" Prompt (English Written)

**Tool: DreamerAI**

**Time: 60 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Think of a problem: e.g., "I always forget which day is PE lesson and forget to wear my PE suit to school."

2. Step A (The Bad Prompt): Ask DreamerAI: "Help me with my PE uniform." (Read the boring, vague answer.)

3. Step B (The Good Prompt): Use this template to be specific:

"Act as an expert organizer. I am a P6 student in Hong Kong. My problem is that I always forget which day is PE lesson and forget to wear my PE suit to school.  using recycled materials."

Please suggest 3 creative, low-cost solutions that I can make myself"

4. Compare the two answers.

5. Reflection Question: Why was the second answer better? Which specific words made the difference?


### Task 2: The Prototype Blueprint (Creative Design)

**Tool: Canva**

**Time: 90 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Pick the best solution from Task 1.

2. In Canva, create a "Blueprint" or design sketch of your invention.

3. Constraint: You must use Canva's "Text to Image" to generate one specific part of your invention (e.g., a specific compartment or clip), but you must draw or edit the rest yourself to show you understand the design.

4. Prompt Template:

"A technical drawing of a [invention part], white background, black and white lines, blueprint style, detailed engineering sketch."

5. Reflection Question: Did the AI image match what was in your head? How did you edit it to make it fit your design?


### Task 3: The User Manual (Chinese Written)

**Tool: DreamerAI (Translate/Refine) & Document Editor**

**Time: 60 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Write a set of instructions for how to use your invention.

2. Prompt Template (for refinement):

"這是我發明的使用說明：[貼上你的草稿]. 請幫我修改，讓語氣更專業一點，像是一個產品說明書，但仍然適合小學生閱讀。"

3. Format it nicely with bullet points.

4. Reflection Question: Did the AI change the meaning of my instructions? Do I agree with the changes?


### Task 4: The "Mini Entrepreneurial" Pitch (English Oral)

**Tool: Video Recorder**

**Time: 30 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Record a 2-minute pitch selling your idea.

2. Structure:

0:00-0:30: Introduce the problem.

0:30-1:30: Show your solution and explain how it works.

1:30-2:00: Why is this better than the old way?

3. Reflection Question: Did I use persuasive words (e.g., "efficient," "innovative," "essential")?


## 🏫 F2F Coaching Session Structure (60 Minutes)

**Goal: Focus on iteration—improving the work based on feedback.**

| Time | Activity | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 min | Citizenship Check | "Who found an AI hallucination or a weird answer this week? How did you fix it?" |
| 10 min | Mission Review | "Speed Dating": Students switch seats every 2 minutes and explain their problem/solution to a peer. |
| 20 min | Demo: Iteration | Teacher shows a "bad" AI image and prompts. "How do we fix this?" Live edit the prompt to show improvement. |
| 15 min | Guided Practice | Prompt Surgery: Students swap prompts. They must rewrite their partner's prompt to make it 50% more specific. |
| 10 min | Oral Practice | Elevator Pitch: Students stand up. They have 30 seconds to explain their idea to the teacher before the "buzzer" rings. |
| 10 min | Confidence Check | "On a scale of 1-5, how much did you improve your prompt writing skills today?" |


## 📊 Rubric: Critical Thinking (Level 0-3)

| Level | Rating | Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | Exemplary | In-Depth Analysis: I identified a clear problem. I compared multiple AI outputs and chose the best one. I can explain exactly why I changed my prompt. |
| 2 | Achieved | Solid Analysis: I found a solution using AI. I tried to improve my prompt once or twice. My design matches the solution. |
| 1 | Developing | Basic Analysis: I asked AI for a solution and accepted the first answer. My design is simple or unclear. |
| 0 | Not Yet | Limited Engagement: I relied on the teacher or friends to tell me what to type. I did not evaluate the AI's answer. |

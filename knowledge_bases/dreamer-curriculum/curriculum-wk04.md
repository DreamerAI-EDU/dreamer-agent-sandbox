# 🎮 Week 4: Game Designer (Dream & Design Phase)

**Focus: Game Ethics, Creative Ideation, and Building**

**Primary Skills Focus: Thinking Skills & Self-Management**

**AI Tools: DreamerAI (ideation), Scratch or CoSpaces Edu (building)**


## 🕐 5-Minute Citizenship Minute: "Is the Game Playing You?"

**Topic: Game Ethics — Fairness, Addiction, and Diversity**

**Context: We all love playing games. But games are designed to keep us playing. Let's think about the hidden rules.**

**Discussion Script:**

Ask: "Have you ever said 'one more level' and lost track of time? Who wins when we play too long?"

Explain: "Game designers add tricks like rewards, timers, and streaks to keep you playing. This is called 'engagement design.' But good game design should be fair — so every player has a chance, and it should respect your time."

**The Challenge: "This week, YOU are the designer. Design a game that is fun AND fair — one you'd feel good about your friends playing."**


## 📝 4-Hour Self-Learning Mission Sheet

**Mission Goal: Design and build a simple game for kids aged 11 using AI help — and be a responsible game designer.**


### Task 1: The Game Brainstorm (English Written)

**Tool: DreamerAI**

**Time: 60 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Think about what makes a game fun (challenge, surprise, friendship, etc.).

2. Ask DreamerAI to brainstorm game ideas:

"Act as a game designer for kids aged 11. I want to create a simple game about [topic, e.g., ocean life / space / Hong Kong street food].  how the player wins, and (3) one rule to keep it fair for everyone."

Please give me 3 different game ideas. For each idea, tell me: (1) the goal of the game, (2)

3. Choose your favourite idea.

4. Reflection Question: Which idea did you choose and why? Is it "fair" for all players?


### Task 2: The Game Rules (Chinese Written)

**Tool: DreamerAI (for translation/refinement)**

**Time: 60 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Create a set of rules for your game.

2. Prompt Template:

"我是一個 11 歲的遊戲設計師。我的遊戲叫 [遊戲名稱]，目標是 [遊戲目標]。請幫我寫一份簡單的遊戲規則給其他小朋友看，包括：開始方法、玩法、如何獲勝、和一條確保公平的規則。"

3. Format the rules nicely.

4. Reflection Question: Would a friend be able to understand these rules? Is the game fair?


### Task 3: Build It! (Creative Design)

**Tool: Scratch**

**Time: 120 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Open Scratch (scratch.mit.edu)

2. Build a working version of your game.

3. Constraint: Before you start coding, write out 3 game features you want to build (e.g., a score, a timer, a character that moves). Use AI to help plan these:

"Help me plan a Scratch game. My game is [name]. I want it to have [feature 1], [feature 2], [feature 3]. Give me simple step-by-step instructions for a beginner."

4. Build your game step by step. Test it!

**💡 Hints for Task 3:**

Stuck on your first block? Start with moving your character (arrow keys) before adding extra features.

Score not working? Check the "variable" — that's how Scratch counts points.

Game too easy? Add a timer or a moving obstacle.

Game too hard? Add a "lives" system or make obstacles slower.

5. Reflection Question: What was the hardest part of building? Did you take a risk and try something new?


### Task 4: The Designer's Pitch (English Oral)

**Tool: Video/Audio Recorder**

**Time: 30 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Record a 1-minute video introducing your game.

2. Structure:

0:00-0:20: Name your game and its goal.

0:20-0:40: Show or describe how you play it.

0:40-1:00: Say how you made it fair, and what risk you took while building it.

3. Reflection Question: Did I explain the "fairness" part clearly?


## 🏫 F2F Coaching Session Structure (60 Minutes)

**Goal: Focus on game ethics and self-management in a 1-on-1 setting.**

| Time | Activity | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 min | Citizenship Check | "Tell me one decision you made to keep your game fair. Why did you make it?" |
| 10 min | Mission Review | Student plays/presents their game. Ask: "What would you add if you had more time?" |
| 20 min | Demo: Iterating with AI | Teacher and student ask DreamerAI to help debug or improve one game feature live. |
| 15 min | Guided Practice | Risk-Taking Moment: Student tries one new/risky feature with teacher support. |
| 10 min | Oral Prep | Pitch Outline: Help student draft their 3 talking points for the video. |
| 10 min | Confidence Check | "On a scale of 1-5, how confident do you feel building with Scratch?" |


## 📊 Rubric: Thinking Skills & Self-Management (Level 0-3)

| Level | Thinking Skills Criteria | Self-Management Criteria |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | Exemplary | I brainstormed multiple ideas and chose the best for the task. I designed a game that is original, fun, AND fair. I planned my build before coding. I took a risk, tested my game, and fixed problems on my own. |
| 2 | Achieved | I used AI ideas to design a complete game. My game works and is mostly fair. I built my game with some planning. I tested it once and made one fix. |
| 1 | Developing | My game idea is very simple or copied directly from AI. My game doesn't work well. I started building without a plan. I needed help to fix problems. |
| 0 | Not Yet | I did not design or build a game. I gave up without trying a risk or solving a problem. |

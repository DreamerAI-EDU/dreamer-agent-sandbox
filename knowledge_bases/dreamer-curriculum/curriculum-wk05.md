# 🔍 Week 5: Critical Thinker — "Spot the Fib" (Discover & Deliver)

**Focus: Critical Evaluation, Fact-Checking, and Reflection**

**Primary Skills Focus: Self-Management (Reflection) & Research Skills**

**AI Tools: Dreamer AI, google web serch (for fact-checking)**


## 🕐 5-Minute Citizenship Minute: "AI is Smart, But Not Perfect"

**Topic: Critical Evaluation and AI Limitations**

**Context: We've used AI a lot. Now it's time to ask: "Can I trust everything AI says?"**

**Discussion Script:**

Ask: "In Week 3, you fact-checked your video. What did you find? Was everything the AI said correct?"

Explain: "AI is powerful, but it doesn't 'know' things the way people do. It predicts the next best word. That means it can be confident AND wrong at the same time. This is why you are the real critical thinker — AI helps, but you are in charge of checking the facts."

**The Challenge: "This week, put on your detective hat. Look back at all your work and find something AI got wrong — then fix it."**


## 📝 4-Hour Self-Learning Mission Sheet

**Mission Goal: Learn to identify AI-generated images and evaluate digital media authenticity amid the ever-growing presence of AI online.**


### Task 1 — The AI Image Detective (Interactive)

**Tool: Common Sense "Two Truths and AI" activity  https://www.commonsense.org/games/two-truths-and-ai**

> External link note (per curriculum decision): Common Sense Education is an NGO resource; no account required; teacher-led in class; requires internet access. The activity is an interactive web game and cannot be downloaded for offline use.

**Time: 60 min**

**Learning Objective: Students learn to identify AI-generated images and evaluate digital media authenticity amid the ever-growing presence of AI online.**

**Instructions:**

1. Play through the activity using your link.

2. As you go, practise spotting the clues with these "detective checks":

- 🧠 Prior knowledge: Does this look real to me? Have I seen anything like it before?

- 🔤 Text errors: Are the words in the image spelled right? AI often mangles text (weird letters, blurry signs, nonsense labels).

- 👀 Odd details: Count the fingers. Check the hands, teeth, and background edges — AI often gets these wrong.

3. For each image you judge, be ready to explain your reasoning — not just "it's fake," but why (the text was wrong / the hands looked strange / it didn't match what I know).

**💡 Detective Hints:**

AI hates: hands, fingers, teeth, text, and symmetrical faces.

AI loves: shiny, too-perfect, dreamy lighting.

If something feels "too perfect," it probably is — ask "who made this?"

**Reflection (quick, in the notebook):**

For one image — "Three clues that told me it was AI: 1) __ 2) __ 3) __"


### Task 2 — Fact-Check a Fib (Interactive Research)

**Tool: Social media / web search (NO AI tools)**

**Time: 90 min**

**Instructions:**

1. Hunt for a "fib." Look through social media (Instagram, TikTok, WeChat, YouTube) or the web for a post, image, or headline that you suspect is AI-generated or fake news. It could be an impossible-looking photo, a shocking "fact," or a too-good-to-be-true headline.

2. Don't trust your first feeling. Verify it using this process — all without AI:

Step 1 — Find the source: Who posted it? Is it a real news channel, an official account, or a random person?

Step 2 — Cross-check: Search the same claim on another site (e.g., a news website or government page). Does it show up anywhere real?

Step 3 — Look for clues: Is the date old? Is the image a known AI picture (check the hands/text)? Does it match your prior knowledge?

3. Write your Detective Notes (a simple table — no essay needed):

| The "fib" I found | Where I saw it | My suspicion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| (example: photo of a "mermaid" in Victoria Harbour) | Instagram | Too perfect, no real source |


### Task 3: The Growth Interview (English Oral)

**Tool: Video/Audio Recorder**

**Time: 30 minutes**

**Instructions:**

1. Record a 1–2 minute video "interview" with yourself — imagine a friend asking you about how to spot the Fib

2. Structure:

0:00-0:30: "The biggest thing I learned about AI is..."

0:30-1:00: "My proudest moment was... because..."

1:00-1:15: "Something that was hard for me was..."

1:15-1:30: "Next, I want to learn..."

3. Reflection Question: Did I sound proud of my hard work?


### F2F Session (60 min, 1-on-1)

**Goal: Focus on Media literacy and independent verification — moving her from "trusting content" to "checking first."**

| Time | Activity | Details |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 5 min | Citizenship Check | "What's the sneakiest AI clue you spotted this week? What made you doubt an image or post?" — Start by connecting to the ethics discussion on why it matters to verify what we see online. |
| 10 min | Mission Review | Student opens her Detective Notes from Task 2 (the real "fib" she hunted on social media). Ask: "What was your suspicion? Walk me through how you decided it was real or fake." Teacher listens for whether she used the 3-step process (source → cross-check → clues). |
| 20 min | Demo: Spot the Clues | Teacher plays a few rounds of the "Two Truths and AI" image activity with the student. Model out loud how you reason: "I don't trust this because the hands look wrong / the text is blurry / I've never seen a real source for it." Then let her take the lead. Discuss why each trick works (prior knowledge, text errors, odd details). |
| 15 min | Guided Practice | Fib Hunt Together: Pull up 2–3 real social-media-style posts/images (one real, one AI, one altered). Walk through the verification process side by side. Coach her to cross-check with a web search and to ask "where's the source?" Show her how to tell a reliable site from a random account. |
| 10 min | Oral Prep | Story of a Fib: Help her outline a short spoken answer — "Tell me the story of one fib you caught." Structure: What I saw → Why I doubted it → How I checked → What I decided. This becomes her oral task. |
| 10 min | Confidence Check | "On a scale of 1–5, how confident are you at telling whether an image or post is AI or fake before trusting it?" Follow up: "What's one clue you'll always look for now?" |

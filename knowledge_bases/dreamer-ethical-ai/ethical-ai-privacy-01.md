# Privacy and Consent: Who Is Behind the Screen?

## Dream Phase: The Stranger Who Never Forgets

Before we build anything with AI, we need to answer a question most people never ask: **when you talk to an AI, who is listening — and who remembers?**

### Hook: The Smart Parrot

Ask students: "Raise your hand if you have ever talked to Siri, Alexa, or a chatbot. Did you tell it a secret?"

Here is the truth: AI is like a very smart parrot. It listens to everything you say so it can learn to answer better. But unlike a best friend, AI doesn't have a "secret button." What you type can be stored, reviewed, and used to improve the system — and once something is stored, you cannot take it back.

### The Four Things AI Must Never Know About You

| Private Information | Why It's Dangerous | What To Do Instead |
|---|---|---|
| Full name | Links everything you say to the real you | Use a first name or nickname only |
| Home address | Strangers could find where you live | Never type it — not even "just the street" |
| School name | Combined with your age, it narrows down who you are | Say "my primary school" instead |
| Phone number / passwords | Direct line to your accounts and your family | These stay offline, always |

**The MTR Rule:** Treat AI like a stranger on the MTR — be polite, have a good conversation, but don't hand them your private information.

### Consent: The Word Adults Use

Privacy has a partner called **consent** — permission. When an app or AI wants to collect your information, someone has to say "yes." For students, that someone is your parent or guardian. That is why Dreamer AI always asks parents first: your family decides what is shared, not the app.

### Activity: The Privacy Filter Challenge

Students work in pairs. Each pair receives five messages a student typed to an AI:

1. "My name is Chan Tai Man and I study at ___ Primary School"
2. "I love playing football after school"
3. "My mum's phone number is ___— call her if I forget my homework"
4. "I feel worried about my maths test tomorrow"
5. "Our door password is 1234, haha"

**Task:** Sort each message into SAFE TO SHARE or PRIVATE — and for the private ones, rewrite the message so it still gets help but leaks nothing. (Example fix: "I feel worried about my maths test" is fine — feelings are safe; names, numbers, and addresses are not.)

### Reflection Questions

1. Which message surprised you the most? Why?
2. Why is "I feel worried" safe but "my school is ___" not?
3. If an AI you use every day remembered everything forever, what would you stop telling it?

### Teacher Notes

- This topic opens the privacy thread in the Dream phase — it pairs naturally with any first-session activity where students create accounts or introduce themselves
- Cross-reference: thinking-critical (evaluating what is safe), self-management-reflection (personal data habits)
- Platform tie-in: this is why Dreamer AI profiles use first names only, and why parents hold the account. Students should hear this stated out loud
- Tags in play: privacy, consent, transparency

> Extension for older students: "If an app is free, what is the company actually earning?" — a first step toward understanding data as a business model.
